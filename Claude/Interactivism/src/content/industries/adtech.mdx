## The density is the problem

AdTech interfaces carry more data per screen than almost any other software category — bid landscapes, inventory forecasts, pacing, attribution windows, brand safety controls, margin. The instinct is to expose all of it, because someone on the team needs every field.

What that produces is a product only its authors can operate: onboarding measured in weeks, a sales team that can't run a demo without a solutions engineer in the room, and a support burden that scales with revenue instead of flattening against it.

The work is deciding what each role actually needs in view at the moment of decision — and having the discipline to move everything else one layer back.

## Self-serve is a design problem before it's a go-to-market problem

Every platform eventually wants to move down-market. The managed-service revenue is real but it doesn't compound, and the smaller advertisers are where the growth is.

That transition usually stalls in the product, not the pricing. An interface built for a trained trader who lives in it daily does not survive contact with a marketing manager who opens it twice a month. Self-serve requires different defaults, different guardrails, and a different tolerance for how much a user should have to understand before their first campaign goes live.

## We've watched the industry change shape three times

Overture invented paid search. OpenX and PubMatic built the programmatic supply chain that replaced it. tvScientific brought performance measurement to streaming television. We've done product work across all three eras — which means we arrive already knowing your vocabulary, your constraints, and which of your problems are genuinely novel versus which the industry has solved before.
