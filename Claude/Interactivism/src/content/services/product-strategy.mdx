## Product Definition

Most product problems are really definition problems: the team is building confidently toward a target that hasn't been validated. We work with founders and product leaders to sharpen the definition of what the product is, who it's for, and what winning looks like — before roadmaps are written and engineers are assigned. This isn't a process exercise; it's the work that makes everything downstream faster.

## Roadmap Planning

A roadmap is a hypothesis about what matters, sequenced by what you know now. We help teams build roadmaps that are honest about uncertainty: clearly prioritized, tied to outcomes rather than features, and structured so that new information can update the plan without a quarterly crisis. The format depends on the team's stage — a seed-stage startup needs a different artifact than a Series B product org.

## Market and Competitive Positioning

Where you play determines what you build. We run competitive analysis, market segmentation, and positioning workshops that help teams make explicit choices about their market rather than letting those choices get made by default. The output is a positioning framework that informs product decisions, not just marketing copy.

## Futurecasting

Some decisions require looking further out than a quarterly roadmap permits. We've done futurecasting work for organizations — including Hitachi and NASA — that needed to understand how technology trends, user behavior shifts, and infrastructure changes would reshape their products and markets over a 5–10 year horizon. The deliverable is a concrete, specific vision of the future: not a trends report, but a narrative that gives leadership something to build toward.

## Stakeholder Alignment

Strategy work fails when it doesn't travel. We design workshops, presentations, and documentation that build alignment across product, engineering, design, and leadership — not by glossing over disagreements, but by making them explicit and resolvable. Teams that leave a strategy engagement pointed in the same direction ship faster and with less internal friction.
